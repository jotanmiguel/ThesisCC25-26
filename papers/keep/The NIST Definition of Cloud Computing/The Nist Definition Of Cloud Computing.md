### The NIST Definition of Cloud Computing (P. Mell, T. Grance, 2011)

**Problem Statement:**  
The paper addresses the need for a clear, standardized definition of cloud computing, in a context where the term was being inconsistently used across academia, government, and industry.
This issue is relevant because the absence of a unified definition was leading to confusion in technological development, regulation, and procurement of cloud services.

**Objectives:**  
The authors aim to provide a concise, authoritative definition and taxonomy of cloud computing.
Specifically, they seek to identify its essential characteristics, service models, and deployment models, offering a common vocabulary for researchers, system architects, and policymakers.

**Proposed Solution:**  
The proposed framework defines cloud computing as “a model for enabling ubiquitous, convenient, on-demand network access to a shared pool of configurable computing resources”.
It decomposes the concept into:
- Five essential characteristics: on-demand self-service, broad network access, resource pooling, rapid elasticity, and measured service.
- Three service models: Software as a Service (SaaS), Platform as a Service (PaaS), and Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS).
- Four deployment models: private, community, public, and hybrid cloud.
This taxonomy provides a flexible yet structured model applicable across contexts.

**Evaluation:**  
The document is not an empirical study but a conceptual standard (NIST SP 800-145).
Its evaluation is implicit: its adoption by U.S. federal agencies and international institutions demonstrates its validation and relevance as a foundational reference for cloud computing taxonomy and interoperability.

**Key Contributions:**  
- Established the definitive NIST definition of cloud computing, widely adopted in academia and industry.
- Introduced the five essential characteristics and the three-tier service model (SaaS, PaaS, IaaS).
- Provided the first standardized classification of deployment models (private, public, community, hybrid).
- Offered a reference model for government and enterprise cloud adoption policies.

**Limitations and Future Work:**  
The definition focuses on conceptual and operational abstraction, not on implementation specifics or security frameworks.
Future work (later NIST publications such as SP 500-292 and SP 800-146) expanded on architecture, interoperability, and risk management.

**Relevance to Current Project:**  
This publication establishes the conceptual foundation for the DI cloud infrastructure project, defining the principles that guide design and implementation.
In particular:
- On-demand self-service and measured service inform automation and resource tracking.
- IaaS serves as the operational model targeted by the project.
- The private cloud deployment model aligns with the goal of building an internal infrastructure for FCUL’s Department of Informatics.
